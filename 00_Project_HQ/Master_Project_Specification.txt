# Section 2 â€“ Problem Statement

## 2.1 Background

Small businesses such as grocery stores and retail shops play a significant role in the economy. However, many of these businesses continue to rely on manual methods, spreadsheets, or basic billing software for inventory and sales management. These approaches make it difficult to monitor stock levels accurately and respond effectively to changes in customer demand.

As a result, inventory planning often depends on the experience and intuition of the business owner rather than data-driven decision-making.

---

## 2.2 Problem Statement

Traditional inventory management systems primarily focus on recording stock transactions without providing intelligent insights into future demand. This can lead to several operational challenges, including:

* Overstocking of products, increasing storage costs and wastage.
* Stock shortages, resulting in lost sales and dissatisfied customers.
* Difficulty in identifying future demand trends.
* Time-consuming manual analysis of historical sales records.
* Limited decision support for inventory planning and replenishment.

Although large enterprise ERP systems provide advanced forecasting capabilities, they are often expensive and overly complex for small businesses.

---

## 2.3 Proposed Solution

The proposed system, **AI-Based Inventory & Sales Forecasting System for Small Business ERP Management**, addresses these challenges by integrating essential ERP functionalities with an AI-based demand forecasting module.

The system records inventory and sales information, analyses historical sales data, predicts future product demand, and provides forecasting results that support inventory planning. The forecasting module is intended to assist business owners in making informed decisions rather than replacing human judgment.

---

## 2.4 Expected Benefits

The proposed solution aims to:

* Improve inventory planning.
* Reduce stock shortages and overstocking.
* Support data-driven business decisions.
* Simplify inventory management for small businesses.
* Demonstrate the practical integration of Artificial Intelligence into a lightweight ERP system suitable for academic and real-world applications.

---

## 2.5 Scope Limitation

Version 1.0 of the project focuses on selected ERP functionalities together with AI-based sales forecasting. Advanced enterprise modules such as Human Resource Management, Payroll, Supplier Management, and comprehensive accounting are intentionally excluded to maintain a realistic scope for a single-developer undergraduate project.

# Section 12 â€“ Model Evaluation Strategy

## 12.1 Overview

To ensure that the forecasting module provides reliable predictions, the model will be evaluated using historical backtesting and standard forecasting performance metrics. This evaluation demonstrates the model's predictive capability using real historical data rather than subjective judgement.

---

## 12.2 Backtesting Methodology

The forecasting model will be validated using **historical backtesting**.

The historical sales dataset will be divided into two parts:

* **Training Data:** Used to train the forecasting model.
* **Validation Data:** Historical records withheld during training and later used to compare predicted values with actual sales.

For example, if sales data is available from January to December, the model may be trained using January to September data and then used to predict October to December. These predictions are compared against the actual recorded sales for those months.

---

## 12.3 Evaluation Metrics

The forecasting performance will be assessed using:

### Mean Absolute Error (MAE)

Measures the average absolute difference between predicted and actual values.

Lower MAE indicates better forecasting performance.

---

### Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE)

Measures the average percentage error between predictions and actual observations.

Lower MAPE indicates higher prediction accuracy.

---

### Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) *(Optional)*

May be included as an additional evaluation metric to measure larger prediction errors.

---

## 12.4 Visual Evaluation

Model performance will also be demonstrated using graphical comparisons, including:

* Actual Sales vs Predicted Sales
* Historical Sales Trend
* Forecast Visualization

These graphs provide an intuitive understanding of forecasting performance.

---

## 12.5 Interpretation

The objective of the forecasting model is not to predict every future value perfectly. Instead, the goal is to generate sufficiently accurate forecasts that improve inventory planning and support business decision-making.

Forecast accuracy will therefore be evaluated using recognised forecasting metrics rather than expecting exact matches between predicted and actual sales.

---

## 12.6 Academic Justification

The use of historical backtesting together with MAE and MAPE follows widely accepted practices in demand forecasting research and provides an objective method for validating the forecasting model.
