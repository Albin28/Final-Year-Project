Abstract
Product demand prediction is highly important as levels of inventory, customers forecast accuracy and overall 
performance are directly impacted by this demand planning. As the main problem of this research, accurate forecasting 
as a crucial challenge for achieving high performance is targeted. This paper has developed a demand planning 
framework with analytical capabilities of sales and consumer patterns, historical sales, and seasonality data to maximize 
the company’s ability to satisfy consumer demand. The framework has been implemented in an empirical business 
environment and has been analyzed from a practical viewpoint. A comprehensive picture of all factors is acquired from 
this viewpoint for successful implementation. The primary objective of the framework is to enhance the demand planning 
process in joint forecast project and how these improvements will help other business units, a crucial component of the 
supply chain, to achieve greater performance levels. As a result, the process of demand planning is examined from a 
system viewpoint for its integration with the supply chain E2E process, performance, forecasting methods, and logistical 
structure which serve as the foundation for assessment of the current condition and context of the new system. A 
deeper focus is placed on the analysis of the demand planning processes and forecasting techniques. Several root 
causes are found based on the customer forecast inputs like forecast horizon, forecasting methods, standard process 
with customers, performance measurement and communication with customers. Integration of JDA, ERP, and other data 
sources, combined with flexible forecasting tools, greatly refines the demand planning while enhancing overall decision 
making. By blending statistical models with real-time customer insights, the firms maintain an agile, efficient supply chain 
that can quickly adapt to market disruptions.
Article Highlights
•	 Demand planning can be improved by integrating customer inputs into forecasting models.
•	 A robust analytical framework for consolidating JDA, ERP, and customer data to refine supply chain performance.
•	 Investigating through an empirical study the enhancement of automated forecasting tool accuracy, inventory 
alignment, and decision-making.
•	 Offering insights into refining joint forecasting by emphasizing flexible time fences.

1  Introduction
Customer satisfaction and reliability are important elements in every aspect of business [1]. Demand planning examined 
as a part of the broader Sales and Operations Planning (S&OP) process is a vital part of the supply chain because it enables 
companies to satisfy consumer needs with sufficient inventory levels while keeping costs in check [2, 3]. Organizations 
may also improve customer satisfaction by including consumers in the process and considering their opinions [4]. 
However, while demand planning is a crucial component of every business strategy, it has faced challenges for its 
efficiency in empowering businesses in foreseeing and planning for future demand [5, 6]. This is due to reasons like 
the methods and systems that store the necessary information may not have all the features, which might make the 
job of demand planners challenging [7]. Planning systems may become the next problem impeding the S&OP process 
improvement after it has been successfully deployed in a corporation [8]. Demand planning methods usually must adapt 
to the limitations of information systems [9, 10]. Demand planning is challenging because of market uncertainty, but 
Artificial Intelligence (AI) can make it more effective [11]. These services are provided by a variety of solutions available 
on the market, but AI may be superior to them because it allows for better prediction at scale without bias or human 
mistakes. By forecasting future requests based on historical data and existing trends, AI can assist with demand planning 
[12, 13].
Moreover, today’s dynamic business environment necessitates agile strategies to approach the complexities of supply 
chain [14], rapid market demand shifts [15] and environmental sustainability requirements [16]. Companies and their 
supply chain stakeholders have to face diverse product portfolios and geographically dispersed operations, which require 
sophisticated forecasting methods to remain competitive [17]. Emerging technological trends, such as AI-driven analytics 
for rapid product development [18], distributed ledger technologies for supply chain configuration [19] and effective 
production and inventory planning [20] hold the potential to strengthen supply chain resilience. Inaccurate forecasts lead 
to misaligned inventory levels and result in escalation of warehouse costs and customer dissatisfaction [8]. Consequently, 
improving the forecasting capabilities is inevitable for reducing risks and supporting the proper resource allocation 
and sustaining a competitive edge [3]. By integrating emerging technologies and more effective customer insights, 
this research aims to offer improved strategies for managing the complex challenges in demand planning and focused 
challenges like: 
1.	 Poor visibility: Inability to perceive the big picture as demand planners cannot see everything. They can not take a 
big difference in market to foresee future trends because they can only observe what is occurring in their product 
sector [21, 22].
2.	 Poor forecasting: Forecasting mistakes by demand planners frequently lack precise knowledge of production capacity, 
or the amount of inventory required in the approaching seasons [6, 23].
3.	 Poor Consistency: Inconsistency of demand planners are unable to adhere to a standard demand planning procedure. 
Each business has its own set of procedures, which causes inconsistent data inputs and outputs along with complex 
analytics models [24, 25].
To fulfill the mentioned difficulties, the research studies suggest that the demand fulfillment must be data-driven and 
use big data technologies to be effective [3, 11, 13, 26]. The use of big data for demand fulfillment is one of the most 
current developments, which has been used in a variety of sectors [27]. Large volumes of consumer data may be analyzed 
using AI to produce insights that improve fulfillment. Moreover, S&OP and, more specifically, demand planning, are the 
challenges that many companies face [28]. The choice of information sources presents a typical problem to demand 
planning in a complex and global context where many businesses operate [29]. The planning process frequently involves 
a significant number of people, which poses difficulties for information flow, supporting procedures, and systems, as 
well as for the importance, accuracy, and applicability of the information, especially when it comes to a practical and 
operational business environment [30].
The evolving customer demands and the increasingly complex supply chain environment necessitate enhancements 
in forecasting processes [31]. This can be targeted by proposing a joint forecasting initiative that considers real-time 
customer inputs with automated predictive tools to reduce both the risk of overstock and stockouts while strengthening 
customer service levels. This requires improved data consolidation, heightened forecast accuracy, and scalable demand 
planning for improving the supply chain agility and long term competitiveness [32]. Standardizing forecast submission

1.1  Problem description and research aims
The business case focused on in this paper has a structure for demand planning that uses a collaborative customer fore-
cast with a time horizon of 3–6 months to increase the forecast accuracy. However, it is challenging to attain a higher fill 
rate due to the difference between forecast values and order quantities produced by the customers. The company must 
adhere to the customer-provided forecast numbers if the risks are greater, which eventually leads to a lower fill rate. In 
this case, responding to the customer demand will be a challenging task. Customers whose forecasted demand is too low 
but place high orders will cause the business to experience poor fill rates and excessive cuts, which will ultimately result 
in losses for both the company and the customer. Similarly, forecasting high demand and ordering low amounts leads 
to inventory costs [33]. The process of demand planning is meant to be the focus of this structure’s perspective, while 
the Supply Chain and logistical organization are also considered. Technical, organizational, economic, and social factors 
will all be taken into consideration. In consideration of this, the following main question for this research is formulated:
•	 Examining the Root cause of the customer’s orders that differs from what was forecasted, What is the impact of these 
root-causes?
•	 How the demand planning process can be improved to prevent the mentioned problems and enhance performance?
2  Literature review
2.1  Strategic planning and decision making
Strategic planning and decision-making are widely mentioned as some of the most crucial responsibilities of a company’s 
board and management, but it has been discovered that senior management frequently lacks these skills. Some of these 
decisions might be related to long-term goals, while others might be related to short-term goals. These choices could be 
tied to short-term objectives in some cases and long-term objectives in others. The board must ensure that while making 
decisions, they are considering every aspect of the company. They must consider what is beneficial to the company as 
a whole and not just for one division or department [34].
Strategic decisions can be accomplished in a variety of ways and contexts. It consists of:
•	 Analytical method
•	 An intuitive strategy
•	 Participatory methodology
•	 Hierarchical methodology
Demand forecasters create predictions about future consumer demand, keep tabs on historical demand and inventory 
levels, examine sales orders and marketing strategies to anticipate customer needs, spot potential production bottle-
necks, and suggest operational adjustments to boost efficiency. Demand planners frequently must strike a compromise 
between short-term needs and long-term resource allocation estimates. A company’s decision-making will also be 
influenced by the kinds of items it sells. For instance, a company’s decision-making would be more centered on lowering 
expenses than increasing profit margins if it had a large mix of low-margin and high-margin products.
Along with handling international risks like currency fluctuations or political unpredictability, demand planners need 
to find new markets and chances to increase sales. Software for demand planning may be employed as a strong decision-
making tool in this situation since it can not only improve forecast accuracy but also provide information on potential 
external influences on future activities like revenue growth or customer satisfaction. The use of AI technology in demand 
planning is becoming more common due to its precise forecasts that assist businesses in making decisions devoid of 
human bias. AI employs data analytics algorithms that can accurately and precisely anticipate future events based on 
historical trends, something that is impossible for humans to achieve [33].

2.2  Demand planning as a component of strategic planning
Companies estimate the demand for their products using the demand planning process. It is based on market information 
that is currently accessible and previous data [17]. Demand management is a practical strategy for adapting to shifting 
market conditions. The ability to estimate product demand and production gives managers the knowledge they need 
to make more informed decisions in the future. Customers and businesses may both benefit from demand planning, 
which has a range of advantages such as increased profitability, customer happiness, and corporate efficiency [21]. The 
warehouse’s inventory levels will be influenced by the organization’s demand strategy, which will also have an impact 
on service levels [35]. This can be an issue since it becomes challenging to make precise estimates for future demand 
when demand levels are changing quickly. There are five primary parts:
•	 Prediction
•	 Improvement
•	 Sourcing
•	 Selection
•	 Review
Demand planning forecasting involves making decisions now as well as in the future to determine what adjustments are 
necessary to make the operations and capital expenditures for future production [36]. The cost of sourcing and select-
ing operations, along with how much inventory should be created or bought at certain points in time, as well as when 
production capacity will become available, are all factors that are considered when forecasting. The level of optimization 
might be global or item-level, and it may also entail modifying either external sources (like suppliers) or internal processes 
(e.g., R&D). Finding possible suppliers that can fulfill the requirements from among those considered suitable based on 
certain criteria is considered sourcing [37].
2.3  Sales and operations planning (S&OP)
S&OP has been developed to meet the requirement of bringing strategy to the everyday life of businesses and to allow 
cross-organizational collaboration as the topic of strategic decision making has shown to be a frequent topic in organiza-
tional study. S&OP’s primary goals are to balance supply and demand as well as create connections between a company’s 
strategic and operational initiatives. The dedication of the participants is one of the crucial success factors of effective 
S&OP. By improving the process for giving stakeholders the essential information, this type of commitment can be 
formed [38]. The key emphasis for businesses that prioritize service differentiation is the relationship between customer 
demands, customer centrality, innovation, and service differentiation. Customer demands are not a significant predic-
tor; however, customer centrality is a strong predictor of service differentiation and innovation. Additionally, it has been 
discovered that the complexity of consumer wants is favorably correlated with service distinctiveness but negatively 
correlated with innovation [39].
The traditional view of S&OP is that it’s a process which is completed monthly. It is important to do this to review the 
company’s performance and make any necessary adjustments. However, there are many ways to complete this pro-
cess, and it should be tailored to fit the needs of the company. The demand S&OP puts a strong emphasis on customer 
demand and aids in matching the supply chain to it. The sales and operations teams collaborate in this process to esti-
mate demand, schedule production, and adjust inventory levels as necessary. The major objective of this procedure is to 
reduce lead times and costs while maintaining customer service standards to optimize inventory levels [40]. The process 
of S&OP is often seen as a five-step process, as illustrated in Fig. 1:

2.4  Conclusion from literature review
One of the essential components of an effective planning process is the demand planning strategy. Although earlier 
industries frequently worked more in a supply-driven manner, the market is shifting to become more customer-
oriented. Customers are in the driving seat because the forecast of the products is determined to meet their 
demands. This is accurate across many sectors that had an impact on how business operations are structured. 
To be able to meet the market’s ever-shifting demands and to take advantage of its opportunities, planning and 
forecasting future demand is essential. Highly accurate predictions and plans may aid businesses in overcoming 
difficulties such as longer lead times for materials to be procured, planning the product range, capacity planning, 
and providing suppliers with more precise estimates to prevent the supply chain bullwhip effect. Due to this, each 
process should be designed exclusively to meet the company’s demand. Along with the degree of plans and the 
frequency of planning, the schedule for the plan should also be considered. The planning schedule is frequently 
determined by the companies. The timescale must be close to two years or more to take strategic decisions into 
account along with tactical ones. This aids in planning the demand for goods by considering the technological 
product frameworks. It is usually not practical to formulate demand plans at the Stock Keeping Unit (SKU) level 
since product portfolios are getting complicated and the portfolio will be changing. A statistical forecast based on 
historical data is helpful to aggregate and disaggregate at multiple levels to forecast the future demand. This can 
involve financial controllers reviewing the sales of a specific business unit or product managers considering global 
data per product line, sales managers finding the sales of a specific region, operations managers reviewing the 
planned demand for a specific factory. This allows an area manager, for instance, to modify the plan at the country 
level and have it implemented at all levels where the business is booming. This enables those engaged to see the 
wider picture and lessens the need for highly developed analytical abilities throughout the forecasting process.
The changes in consumer behavior, the changing nature of the market, the expansion of market share, and vari-
ous types of project information are among the potential judgment adjustments that cannot be observed from 
historical data. Most of the research studies have discovered that judgment modifications made to data baselines 
frequently boost prediction accuracy, particularly when the adjustment is larger and not simply a minor adjust-
ment based. The projections are frequently biased by the judgmental modifications, and the projected statistics 
frequently exceed the actual. The preconception is reduced to determine the change in plan. The planning process 
should be in place before deploying planning systems. To facilitate the sharing of data and the development of the 
plans, the demand planning process and systems should be developed. Some of these systems are straightforward 
databases or software applications. These software make a forecast of future demand based on current data from 
markets and consumers using analytics technologies. Companies frequently commit the mistake of failing to have 
the proper kind of system in place that will facilitate this process. This may cause inefficiencies in forecasting the 
demand, which would fail to meet the customer’s demand.

3.1  Business demand planning and logistical structure
The Business domain in this study includes a multinational company. It manufactures and develops  products, and sells & 
distributes Fast-Moving Consumer Goods (FMCG) products in beauty industry. It has customers in numerous industry sec-
tors. Its regional commercial business units serve as the foundation for the corporate global market reporting structure. 
It offers products to Business-to Business (B2B). The business is growing to the position of market leader. The business 
units focus on two categories of consumer beauty and luxury products.   The production of business firm products and 
regular business activities are achieved through their supply chain. For the benefit of consumers, they produce, market, 
sell, and distribute a variety of products. They still rely on a global network of Third-party Manufacturer (TPM), who cre-
ate about one fifth of the final products.  Additionally, they buy packaging parts that are produced in accordance with 
their design requirements. Raw materials include a higher risk of labor and human rights abuses, and it is considered to 
enable reduction of this risk. As a large international company, it also engages in marketing, offices, retail locations, and 
internet, among other supply chain activities. Based on supplier documentation pertaining to the following four major 
categories and evaluating the environmental and social performance of their suppliers: Sustainable Procurement, Labor 
and Human Rights, Ethics, and Environment.
3.1.1  Application of Tools for demand planning
1.	 JDA: James Donald Armstrong (JDA) is a statistical forecast-based system. Everything which is forecasted by the 
demand planners will be in this system. JDA is linked to the production system but not an end-to-end system. With 
a few exclusions, the studied company will position its forecast in JDA globally. The statistical recommendations 
will be used to compute the regular products. Statistical forecast is calculated based on at least 12 months history. 
The forecast will be more precise as there is more historical data. However, it can have a negative effect in specific 
situations, such as Covid. Every initiative must be placed in JDA. Whatever is placed in JDA, it is subsequently used 
by other side of supply chain such as factory planners for production. The data input aggregates data from vari-
ous sources, including JDA for forecasting and ERP for inventory management. It consolidates historical sales data, 
customer forecasts, and relevant market data to create a comprehensive dataset. In the processing layer, the data 
undergoes transformation and cleansing to ensure quality and consistency. This process involves standardizing 
formats, addressing missing values, and applying relevant business rules.
2.	 MFR: Manugistics Forecast Reporting (MFR) is a reporting system. Demand planners can extract the data from JDA and 
from ERP. The latest Forecast Performance updates in a MFR system. The analytical tool is designed with a modular 
architecture. The data input aggregates data from various sources, including JDA for forecasting and ERP for inventory 
management. It consolidates historical sales data, customer forecasts, and relevant market data to create a com-
prehensive dataset. In the processing layer, the data undergoes transformation and cleansing to ensure quality and 
consistency. This process involves standardizing formats, addressing missing values, and applying relevant business 
rules. The core of the tool, this engine utilizes various forecasting algorithms, including: Statistical Models which are 
traditional time series analysis methods, such as the Lewandowski model, which consider trends, seasonality, and 
historical patterns. The results are presented in an intuitive dashboard format, allowing users to visualize forecast 
accuracy, track performance metrics (such as Mean Absolute Percentage Error 90 (MAPE90)), and assess deviations 
in real-time.
3.	 Prometheus©: The tool for producing content is called Prometheus©. It employs AI technology to create material 
for themes, and in areas where it falls short, it may use an automated bidding mechanism to select the best-fitting 
freelancer. In the studied company, ­Prometheus© is an ordering system for all initiatives for luxury brands. It provides 
offerings through Electronic Marketing Book (eMB).
4.	 ERP: It is a connecting tool to a company as well as to the Customers. The demand planners and customers can enter 
their inputs in this system.

3.2.4  Distribution requirements planning (DRP)
DRP is a systematic process to determine which goods are required, in what quantity, at which location, and when they 
are required. The purpose is to meet demand within the distribution network according to desired coverage parameters. 
The process takes place in JDA. For each location in the distribution network, the DRP calculation takes into account the

Benchmarking - No Historical data for new product launches: Currently, qualitative methodologies are gener-
ally used in the forecasting process for new launches of products. Product launches may be utilized to advertise the 
newest and best products in the range and genuinely put the business on the map. Before developing a new product, 
it is commonly recognized practice to consider what has been successful in the past. To inform next launches, there is 
not any information about previous launches, nevertheless. Because of this, it is challenging for businesses to predict 
what will succeed or fail before introducing a new product. Demand planners discuss the anticipated demand for a 
future time through local meetings at various stages of the forecasting process. To develop calculations to support 
these operations, no historical information about product demand is used. The benefit of using this kind of forecast-
ing for new product releases is that it works well.
Statistical forecasting based on historical data: Over the past decades, significant progress has been achieved 
in creating sales forecasting methods that take market factors into account. In assessments of sales forecasting proce-
dures, however, only slight improvements in sales forecasting performance have been noted. Time series techniques 
are one method to use historical data as a forecasting input. In order to create a forecast, Open model Time Series 
(OMTS) algorithms evaluate the sales history and locate existing trends. The four data patterns that may be identified 
in the historical data are level, trend, seasonality, and noise. The forecast is based on how these patterns interact.
•	 Level is the pattern of sales that would occur in the absence of trends, seasonality, and noise.
•	 A trend is a recurring pattern of sales that might be upward or downward, linear or curved.
•	 A recurring pattern of sales growth or decline is known as seasonality.
•	 Noise is the random fluctuation that the time series approach cannot explain in the sales history.
Due to the complexity and low incremental accuracy, open time series approaches have not found widespread usage 
in industry. In contrast to open approaches, fixed time series techniques are employed by the studied company which 
is simpler since they make a priority assumption about the patterns present in the data (level, trend, seasonality, and 
noise). The demand planners will forecast the demand of the product considering historical data, trends, etc. But the 
quality of the data is one of the most important considerations in statistical forecasting.
iii. Joint forecast with customer collaboration: An essential component of the supply chain is the customer’s par-
ticipation in the demand planning process. Making a demand strategy and keeping track of it both assist. Data from 
a variety of sources, including social media, e-commerce analytics, and weather forecasts, are used in the consumer 
cooperation in the demand planning process. Considering this, a project named Joint forecast has been launched 
in the studied company with consumer involvement. The main objective of the project is customers providing their 
forecast inputs for the following three to six months. This will enable demand planners to adjust the forecast accu-
rately and achieve a better supply fill rate. So, the demand planners can plan the production to ensure that there 
will be enough inventory to meet the demand of the customers by receiving forecast inputs for the future months 
and achieving the target of the company. This initiative stems from the realization that relying solely on historical 
data through statistical models is often insufficient, especially in cases where trends fluctuate or product histories 
are limited. By incorporating real-time customer inputs, demand planners can send timely signals to the supply and 
production teams to ensure enough inventory is available to meet future demand.
The Joint forecasting initiative also helps address external influences indirectly. By gathering real-time forecast 
inputs from customers, demand planners can adapt more quickly to external disruptions. For example:
•	 Customers may signal expected shifts in demand due to market disruptions or price changes.
•	 Their forecasts reflect the reality on the ground, allowing the company to adjust supply levels more accurately, even 
if external factors are at play.
Where external influences are known or expected (e.g., anticipated price hikes or major disruptions), demand planners 
are required to document assumptions upon which they base their adjustments. These assumptions provide a qualitative 
understanding of how external factors might affect future demand, complementing the statistical forecast.

4.3  Discussions regarding the numerical analysis
In analyzing the research outcomes, it is imperative to acknowledge that they originate from numerical exemplifications 
anchored in explicit assumptions concerning market conditions and historical data patterns. External perturbations, 
encompassing a spectrum from geopolitical disturbances to fluctuations in raw material prices, may extend beyond 
the purview of purely statistical or time-series models unless they recur with sufficient frequency to be encapsulated 
within the underlying data. Consequently, certain potential inaccuracies or biases may emerge, particularly when atypi-
cal events or disruptions transpire that markedly deviate from established trends. To mitigate these risks, forecasting 
framework integrates human insights and real-time customer input alongside automated tools. This dual approach not 
only helps address the limitations inherent in purely data driven models but also enables faster adaptation to sudden 
market shifts or emerging disruptions. The Joint Forecasting Initiative, for example, provides immediate feedback from 
customers on expected demand changes, while business intelligence allows planners to manually adjust forecasts in 
response to external factors such as major supply chain issues or price hikes. Despite these mitigating measures, reliance 
on assumptions underscores the importance of ongoing model refinement and robust data collection, ensuring that 
forecasting processes remain agile, accurate, and resilient in the face of evolving market complexities.
4.4  Implications for research and practice
The results can be considered for exploring more robust frameworks that can seamlessly integrate both quantitative and 
qualitative insights, particularly for capturing unpredictable external factors. This includes refining hybrid or ensemble 
modeling techniques to incorporate sudden market shifts, while still leveraging manual intelligence to interpret unique 
or one-off disruptions. In practice, the evolution of automated tools and real-time data sharing should be used together 
with the development of flexible protocols, enabling continuous adjustments to plans as more sophisticated indicators 
become available.
5  Conclusion
This research findings provide potential information sources for demand planning as well as details on how to set up 
the process itself. The primary objective of this research is to enhance the current demand planning process in the Joint 
forecast project executed with customer participation. The ability to produce an acceptable degree of customer partici-
pation and customer service are examples of how a company performs. This dual goal is strongly related to inventory 
levels. Both excess and insufficient stock have an impact on these goals. Receiving customer forecast inputs and analyz-
ing them to actually are the main issues with the Joint Forecast Project. This analysis will make it easier for the demand 
planners to create an accurate demand and properly establish the production level. Therefore, improving the demand 
planning process/tools boosts both organizational performance and customer forecast accuracy. Having a standard 
time horizon for submitting the forecast inputs for all the customers makes the process even better. By leveraging joint 
forecasting initiatives and implementing automated tools, managers can reduce operational risks through precise inven-
tory alignment and support their strategic decision-making with accurate, real-time data. So, organizations not only 
improve supply chain agility and responsiveness but also create a sustainable framework for long term competitiveness 
and scalability. The analytical tool designed as a part of the Joint Forecasting Initiative represents an effective system 
that can integrate various forecasting methodologies and data sources, such as JDA and ERP systems. Through its robust 
architecture, algorithmic foundation, and seamless integration processes, the tool enhances the studied business case 
for forecasting capabilities, ultimately improving supply chain efficiency and responsiveness to market demands. The 
results of the implementation in the studied company have created potentials for the demand planning process with 
significant qualitative benefits like:
