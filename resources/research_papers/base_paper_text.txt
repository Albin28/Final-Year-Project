Abstract
Industries are going through a changing scenario, in which they need to constantly seek to improve their processes and 
increase productivity. One of the factors that contribute to improving the performance of companies is inventory man­
agement, which starts with forecasting demand. This research aimed to compare the Machine Learning models, Random 
Forest and XGBoost, and the classic Exponential Smoothing prediction models, Holt, Holt-Winters and SARIMA Seasonal 
Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average. Identify which model generates the best forecasts for the items of a company 
in the energy sector in southeastern Brazil. The database consisted of 2,244 items. The process of classifying the demand 
was carried out in four types: Irregular, Intermittent, Erratic and Regular. The process of adding financial value was 
applied in three groups: A, B and C. The prediction models were implemented using Spyder (Python 3.11), performing 
13,464 iterations. To measure the performance of the prediction models, it was, the Criteria Importance Through Inter­
criteria Correlation (CRITIC) multicriteria analysis method was used to determine the score of the models based on the 
error measures. The Augmented Dickey-Fuller tests were performed (ADF), Kwiatkowski-Phillips-Schmidt-Shin (KPSS), 
Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) and the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC). The result of the analysis identified 
that, in general, the model that presented the best predictions was Random Forest, followed by ExpSmoothing. The model 
Random Forest presented better forecasts for items with irregular and intermittent demand, Holt-Winters performed better 
for regular items, while items with erratic demand did not present models with significant prominence. It also identified 
that the 82 items with value classification A are subject to a more detailed analysis, due to the high representativeness of 
the value.

2  Fundamentals
2.1  Criteria importance through intercriteria 
correlation
The Criteria Importance Through Intercriteria Correlation 
(CRITIC) method is an analytical approach designed to 
determine objective weights of importance in multicriteria 
decision problem (MCDM) [4].
Unlike other methods that require subjective expert 
judgments, CRITIC draws these weights directly from the 
intrinsic information in the data, based on the variability of 
each criterion and its independence or conflict with the other 
criteria [5].
In addition, it avoids subjective interventions by not 
requiring the analyst to set arbitrary cut-off values to deal 
with highly correlated criteria [4].
2.2  Model selection measures
Error measures are statistical metrics that quantify the dif­
ference between the values projected by a model and the 
actual observed data [6, 7]. They serve to validate the accu­
racy and reliability of forecasts, allowing different methods 
to be compared and the most rigorous model to support 
decision-making [5, 6].
Mean Absolute Error (MAE) is a metric that calculates 
the average magnitude of the absolute differences between 
the values predicted by a model and the actual observed data 
[6–8]. It serves to validate the accuracy and performance 
of forecasting models, allowing you to compare different 
methods to select the projection that is technically more 
faithful to reality [6, 9, 10].
MAPE expresses the average of absolute errors in per­
centage terms in relation to the actual observed values [7, 
8]. Measures the accuracy of forecasting models, making it

statistical criteria for the selection and comparison of mod­

They serve to identify the best parameter configuration, 
penalizing excessive complexity to avoid overfitting error 
[11].
In practice, the aim is to minimize these values in order to 
find the model that explains the data with the least possible 
number of variables. In this way, they ensure the choice of 
the most efficient and statistically valid architecture to sup­
port predictions and decision-making [5].
2.3  Machine learning
Machine Learning is a set of techniques and algorithms that 
allow systems to automatically learn patterns and represen­
tations from raw data [6, 11]. It stands out for its ability to 
self-learn and generalize, surpassing traditional statistical 
models when dealing with nonlinear and complex relation­
ships [5].
Its function is to perform tasks such as demand fore­
casting, item classification and pattern detection, ensuring 
greater accuracy in large volumes of information [7, 9, 12]. 
In short, it serves to transform Big Data processing into 
valuable insights, optimizing industrial and commercial 
processes, and strategic decision-making [6, 9, 13].
2.4  XGBoost
Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) is an advanced and 
efficient implementation of gradient boosting algorithms 
that uses decision tree-based models [7, 9].
It employs sophisticated regularization techniques to pre­
vent overfitting and process large volumes of data in a scal­
able manner, ensuring high technical accuracy [9].
Its purpose is to make robust forecasts in various sec­
tors, such as B2B commercial demand, seasonal time series, 
and industrial processes, surpassing traditional statistical 
models. Due to its fast training and low processing time, it 
serves as an essential tool for real-time control systems and 
strategic decision-making support [7]. 
2.5  Random forest
Random Forest is an ensemble learning algorithm that com­
bines predictions from multiple decision trees to increase 
accuracy and technical robustness [9].
It uses the bootstrapping method to train independent 
trees on different subsets of data, which reduces variance 
and prevents overfitting. Its primary purpose is to process 
high-dimensional data and identify complex patterns [13].
2.6  Exponential smoothing
Exponential time series smoothing methods have two types, 
with trend and/or seasonal pattern and without them. These 
predictions are the weighted averages of previous observa­
tions, with weights decaying exponentially as observations 
age [14].
Simple exponential smoothing is one of the easiest mod­
els to apply to forecasting problems [14].
2.7  Holt model
Charles C. Holt adapted simple exponential smoothing 
to enable forecasting of trended data [15]. Holt’s method 
assumes that datasets have a trend and do not have seasonal­
ity, so it uses two components, level and trend. This method 
is mainly used for linear trends with short to medium fore­
cast periods.
2.8  Holt-Winters model
Holt-Winters is an extension of Holt’s method developed 
by Holt’s student Peter Winters that assumes the existence 
of seasonal and trend variations for data observation [16]. 
Considers level, trend and seasonality with the correspond­
ing parameters alfa (x), beta (B) e gama (y).
The Holt-Winters model is one of the most widely used 
methods for analyzing time series with seasonal and trend 
patterns [14].
2.9  SARIMA and ARIMA model
The Seasonal Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average 
(SARIMA) model can handle non-stationary time series and 
capture trends, seasonality, and other linear information in 
the data, thus enabling modeling and forecasting [17]. 
The seasonal autoregressive integrated moving aver­
age (SARIMA) is a model for forecasting time-series data,

2.10  Classification ABC
ABC analysis is based on the Pareto Principle, defined by 
the Italian economist Vilfredo Pareto, which says that the 
majority values (80% of its value) of a given group come 
from a relatively small portion of its constituents (20% of 
your quantity).
The fundamental inventory management technique used 
to categorize items according to their importance, value, 
and financial impact, it allows organizations to focus their 
resources and control efforts on the most critical items, 
rather than treating all products uniformly, which would be 
economically unfeasible [12].

3.5  Demand forecasting
The objective of this research was to compare demand fore­
casting models for spare parts; the primary evaluation met­
ric was forecasting accuracy. For this reason, the forecasted 
values themselves were not presented in detail; instead, the 
step-by-step execution of the forecasting procedures was 
reported to enable replication and future studies by other 
researchers.
The model comparison process was carried out for the 
entire dataset of 2,244 items. Using Spyder (Python 3.11), 
demand forecasts were generated for each item employing 
the six forecasting models addressed in this research: two 
machine learning models (Random Forest and XGBoost) 
and four traditional models (Exponential Smoothing, Holt, 
Holt–Winters, and SARIMA).
Subsequently, forecast errors were calculated to deter­
mine the most robust models.
In total, 13,464 iterations were performed in Spyder 
(Python 3.11), corresponding to six forecasts for each of the 
2,244 items analyzed.
The historical series was divided into training bases 
(Jan/2020 to Dec/2023), test base (Jan/2024 to Dec/2024) 
and the forecast period was set at 12 steps ahead.

It was possible to achieve the objective of this work, which 
was to compare Machine learning models and classical 
models for forecasting demand for spare items of a com­
pany in the energy sector. The Machine Learning models 
used were Random Forest, XGBoost, and the classic models 
Exponential Smoothing, Holt, Holt-Winters, and SARIMA.
To achieve this objective, it was necessary to establish 
two types of classification. Classification of demand for 
while the use of statistical criteria such as AIC and BIC

classifying their inventories simultaneously by the behav­

(ABC Curve). This approach allows you to target efforts 
surgically, the ABC curve prioritizes where accuracy is 
financially vital.
